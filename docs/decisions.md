# Design decisions

The decisions that shape ChorNMT, why they were taken, and the questions still open. The requirements they serve are in [Requirements](requirements.md); the contract rules that apply them are in [Contract structure](contract-structure.md). A new decision, or a change to one, is recorded here in the same commit as the code.

Each entry: the decision, why, and what it implies.

## Model

**D1. The BPMN XML is a view of the asset.** The on-chain asset is the source of truth; changes go through a JSON delta and `setNodes`/`setRoles`, never through the XML. *Why:* the research question is a choreography governed on-chain; an editable XML would be a second source of truth. *Implies:* the BPMN is always regenerated from `tokenURI`; the exported XML is never edited.

**D2. Identity by name, nodes never deleted.** Nodes and roles are keyed by name; BPMN IDs are not stored. A node is overwritten, never removed, and an asset never clears its name lists. *Why:* names are stable across import, delta, and render, and storage removal adds code to an asset close to the size limit. *Implies:* a delta carries whole nodes in their final state, both endpoints of a changed edge; a clean model needs a new asset.

**D3. `tokenURI` is computed, never stored.** A stateless renderer builds the metadata from the asset state in one call. *Why:* stored metadata would drift from the model and cost storage on every change. *Implies:* render reads the whole model in one `tokenURI` call; the renderer lives outside the asset to keep `ChoreographyNMT` under the size limit.

**D4. No version registry (2026-10-08).** `mintVersion`, `predecessorOf`, and `versionOf` were removed. *Why:* the chain already keeps the history of a model: the `setNodes`/`setRoles` transactions and the `NodesChanged`/`RolesChanged` events of its asset. *Implies:* a past state is read from the events, or by querying the asset at an earlier block.

**D13. A `ChoreographyNMT` is one choreography type (2026-10-08).** Its model is fixed at deploy: the ABI-encoded `InitialModel` stored as the code of a data-only contract (SSTORE2), whose address the NMT keeps as an `immutable`. `mint` takes no model; `_mint` deploys an asset that decodes the model in its constructor, so every instance is born with it. `mintWithInitialModel` and the empty mint were removed. *Why:* an NMT identifies one choreography, not a generic container; keeping the model outside the NMT avoids the bytecode limit (the NMT embeds the asset), and the caller cannot pass a different model. *Implies:* another choreography, or another starting model, is another NMT; the model contract is read only at birth, and every later change is a delta on the asset; the model must fit 24,576 bytes; the model is part of the type, so it is not checked by the policies. Deploying a type costs the model contract once (2,343,146 gas for the paper example), and each paper instance costs about 310,000 gas less than an empty mint plus import.

**D14. Roles are born without accounts (2026-10-08).** The model keeps only the role names; each instance links its accounts or licenses with `setRoles`. *Why:* who plays a role is a decision of the instance and must pass the Creator AND Holder checks, licenses included. *Implies:* a protected role is assigned once: `setRoles` accepts it only while it has no account.

**D15. Every Creator policy rejects one-sided flows (2026-10-10).** In the post-update model, `B` is in `A.outgoing` if and only if `A` is in `B.incoming`; the default `CreatorSmartPolicy` enforces it as a well-formedness rule, without a setter, and the constrained policies inherit it. *Why:* the render rejects an inconsistent model, so an accepted one-sided flow would make the instance undrawable (claim C1); `main` had the rule behind a runtime switch, which D5 rules out. *Implies:* a delta that changes a flow carries both endpoints; the paper delta costs 101,548 gas more (1,764,801 → 1,866,349) and the default Creator policy 471,009 more to deploy.

## Policies

**D5. Policies stand for XACML translations (2026-10-06).** Rules and their configuration are literals in the policy source, tuned to the test and evaluation scenarios; there are no runtime setters. *Why:* the contribution is the XACML to smart-policy translation, and XACML policies carry no mutable configuration; literals are also the cheapest form. *Implies:* a rule change is a new policy, installed through the policy it replaces; rule variants are separate policy contracts.

**D6. Creator AND Holder on every asset decision (2026-10-06).** Both policies approve every change to an asset, at the action check (`evaluate`) and at the domain check (`evaluateNodeUpdate`, `evaluateRoleUpdate`). *Why:* the Creator sets the outer bounds and the Holder can only restrict further; neither approval can override a denial by the other. *Implies:* a second external call per mutator, kept cheap by a permissive Holder that reads nothing.

**D7. The Master decides only at the NMT level (2026-10-08).** It governs `mint`, `transferFrom`, and its own replacement, never a change to a model. `MasterSmartPolicyFrozen` was removed. *Why:* a model change is an instance decision (D6). *Implies:* freezing an instance is not a Master feature (see the open questions).

**D8. Default deny (2026-10-06).** Every policy allows an explicit list of actions and returns `false` otherwise. *Why:* a new function must stay blocked until a policy assigns it. *Implies:* `test:policies` checks that every mutator has an explicit decision.

**D9. Roles are played by participant licenses.** A role is linked once to a `ParticipantMutableAsset` of the required type; the organization changes when the license is sold. *Why:* who plays a role is a transferable right with its own policies, not an address the choreography holder rewrites. *Implies:* the license type is an `immutable` set at mint; the Creator policy reads it through `ChoreographyParticipantAM`.

## Contracts and cost

**D10. Minimum total gas (2026-10-06).** The cost of the whole flow (deploy, mint, import, modify) is kept as low as is safe, and every contract change reports gas before and after. *Why:* costs are reported in the paper evaluation. *Implies:* an optimization never changes an allow/deny outcome.

**D11. One shared view interface per asset kind (2026-10-06).** The asset implements it (`is`); policies and renderers call through it. *Why:* the compiler checks that views and interface agree, instead of ad-hoc partial interfaces.

**D12. Mint with `_mint`, not `_safeMint`.** *Why:* no receiver callback may run before the asset is initialized.

## Experiment and evidence

Settled on 2026-10-10 and being implemented in six steps (decisions, single run, sequence diagrams, charts and BPMN, protocol, guardrails); until a step lands, the current `evaluate:*` commands and reports stay. Requirement E in [Requirements](requirements.md#e-the-experiment-is-documented-by-evidence-produced-by-code) lists the acceptance criteria.

**D16. Evidence comes only from code that ran.** Every number, outcome, table, chart, BPMN drawing, and sequence diagram in the experiment documentation is produced by the experiment run; hand-written text (by a person or an LLM) is narrative only. *Why:* the paper must carry no numeric inaccuracy and no mismatch between text, phases, and what the contracts did. *Implies:* values reach the text through placeholders filled by a generator; derived values are recomputed independently and compared, not trusted.

**D17. One run, eight phases, expected outcomes.** A versioned phase specification (`scripts/experiment/phases.js`) is the single source of the experiment: F1 deploy of the type (BPMN import as a sub-step) and of the license infrastructure (participant NMT, certifier, attribute manager, paper-scenario Creator policy); F2 mint of the instance, born with the paper-scenario Creator policy, its role accounts, and the Master denials; F3 participant licenses issued and the Special Carrier role linked, with the Creator denials; F4 baseline render; F5 delta, one allowed and five denied updates; F6 role sale (change of license holder, permissions checked before and after); F7 transfer of a second instance (a denied and an allowed transfer) and its restriction by the new holder (deny-all); F8 final render and delta versus a new instance with the same roles. For each phase it lists the actors, the transactions, and the expected outcome (allowed, or denied with its revert reason). One script runs it on an ephemeral Hardhat network, on one instance, through the same functions as the CLI commands, and fails if a transaction is missing, out of order, sent by another account, calls another function, or has another outcome. `test:policies` stays as the cross-cutting allow/deny matrix. The experiment does not replace the Creator policy: the instance is born with the one it needs, and whether that policy stays replaceable is an open question. *Why:* the paper workflow and the policy lifecycle ran twice on two networks with different instances, so their evidence did not tell one story. *Implies:* phase titles and order in the protocol come from the specification; each role except the licensed one has its own account (Hardhat 10–13), so no account plays two parts; `evaluate:paper`, `evaluate:policies`, `evaluate:summary`, `evaluate:all` and their reports are removed once the replacement is complete, and the cost of replacing the Creator policy leaves the reports (an explicit parity entry).

**D18. Evidence is versioned and compared.** `evaluation/evidence/` holds the last approved run: per phase, actors and accounts, active policies (address, source, bytecode hash, trust-table row), inputs (path, SHA-256), transactions (caller, function, outcome, revert reason, gas, cost), sequence diagrams, output state, and timings; plus one provenance block (solc and optimizer, Node, Hardhat, gnuplot, repetitions, `ETH_USD_PRICE`). `npm run evidence:update [--out <dir>]` regenerates it; `npm run check:evidence` regenerates into a temporary directory and compares, in the pre-commit hook and in CI. Timings are measured over N repetitions (configurable, default 10 plus one warm-up), reported with median, min, max, and standard deviation, and not compared. *Why:* without a reference, a stale report (as `final-report.generated.md` was) goes unnoticed. *Implies:* the evidence carries no timestamps and no source hash (the comparison already detects a code change); `ETH_USD_PRICE` is fixed; `check:gas` reads its figures from the evidence; files in `evaluation/evidence/` are never edited by hand.

**D19. Sequence diagrams are generated from the call trace; use cases are verified.** The run records the call trace of every transaction (`debug_traceTransaction`) and of the `tokenURI` read of each render (`debug_traceCall`), and the off-chain steps it executes with the hash of what each one writes. `scripts/experiment/sequence.js` generates one sequence diagram per phase (`evaluation/evidence/uml/F<n>`) in two views: complete (`F<n>-full`, every call) and filtered (`F<n>`, for the docs and reports), derived by two rules only: consecutive read-only calls between the same two contracts become one `reads:` message with the count of each function, and policy calls are grouped by the check they answer, named from the function called (`evaluate` Action check, `evaluate…Update` Domain check, an asset's `transferFrom` Transfer check). Each transaction ends with its outcome, revert reason, and gas. The five hand-written sequence diagrams are removed. The two use case diagrams stay hand-written, with their actors named after trust-table columns and played by experiment accounts, and `check:use-cases` checks them both ways: every use case names its trust-table row, evidence transactions decided by that row and sent (when allowed) by one of its actors, and `test:policies` cases; every row that grants an action, and every transaction a policy decides, appears in a use case. A use case the experiment does not exercise is `' evidence: test-only` with the `<<test only>>` stereotype (`setLinked`). The Master and Creator setters have no use case: those policies are never replaced (open question). *Why:* a rendered diagram is not a derived one; a hand-written sequence can show a call that never happens. *Implies:* the generator parses both views back and fails unless the filtered one lists the phase transactions in order with their outcomes, holds exactly the calls of the complete one, and shows the actors of the phase; a sequence diagram is never edited by hand, and a flow changes by rerunning the experiment.

**D20. Charts and BPMN drawings: compare the data, version the image.** All six gnuplot charts are generated by the run; their data files are compared, their images versioned but not compared (gnuplot and font versions change bytes). Every chart point is recomputed from the raw evidence. For BPMN, the XML is compared: the baseline is equivalent to `references/paper-example.bpmn` (same nodes by name and type, flows, roles), the final model equals the baseline plus the delta recomputed from the delta file, the XML equals the on-chain state in the evidence, the SVG contains exactly the element names of the XML, and each image records the hash of the XML it came from. *Why:* an image that does not match its data is a misalignment the reader cannot see. *Implies:* the chor-js SVG check compares normalized text, so a long name split across lines does not fail it.

**D21. The protocol is narrative with placeholders.** `docs/experiment.md`, in English for paper reviewers, holds the case study and the purpose of each phase; tables, figures, sequence diagrams, numbers, and outcome words come from placeholders. A check fails on a number outside a generated block, except declared structural ones listed in a file. Independent consistency checks recompute gas from receipts, phase totals, USD (gas × gwei × price), the delta versus new instance comparison, and the agreement between the test matrix and the run. A permanent parity checklist maps every section, table, column, and artifact of the former reports and of `evaluation/README.md` to its block in the protocol, and fails if one has no destination. *Why:* the protocol must be at least as exhaustive as the reports it replaces, and no section may disappear silently. *Implies:* removing content means removing its checklist entry, visible in the diff.

**D22. Code guarantees, Claude orchestrates.** Determinism lives in npm scripts and CI; hooks only call them (`guard-generated.sh` blocks direct edits to `evaluation/evidence/` and to generated blocks of the protocol; the pre-commit hook runs `check:evidence`). The `experiment` skill, replacing `evaluate`, is the runbook: when to regenerate, how to read an evidence diff, and to stop and ask when an allow/deny outcome changes. `test-runner` reports evidence differences; a read-only `evidence-reviewer` agent checks the narrative for readability and coherence and never produces values. *Why:* an LLM is not deterministic; whatever must be exact is code that runs without Claude.

## Open questions

Not decided yet; the current behavior stays until they are:

- **Who governs transfers.** Today `transferFrom` is decided by the Master (current holder, eligible receiver) and then by the Creator policy of the asset. It may move to another layer.
- **Whether the Master and Creator setters are removed (2026-10-10).** The rule is that the Master and the Creator policy are never replaced: of the choreography type and instances, and of the license type and licenses (the certifier). The setters (`setMasterSmartPolicy`, `setCreatorSmartPolicy`) stay in the contracts for now and `test:policies` keeps covering them; the experiment never calls them, and the use case diagrams do not show them. Whether they are removed, or denied by every policy, is still open.
- **How an instance is frozen.** Not supported today. A Creator variant that denies model changes and that its administrator can replace was considered; it depends on the two questions above.
